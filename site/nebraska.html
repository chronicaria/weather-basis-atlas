Nebraska case study

Nebraska is a compact way to inspect two different sources of weather basis. The study keeps the county-index definition fixed and compares county exposure with the listed weather-station universe and with an additional Nebraska station panel. It is a methodological case study, not an assertion that a specific station is the correct hedge for a particular business.

What is compared

For each supported contract month, the case-study output contains the county degree-day index, the station degree-day indexes, geographic distance, and the same rolling held-out atlas rows used nationally. The comparison distinguishes an index-definition question—how the county area-average differs from a point station—from a proxy-selection question—how a station hedge performs after the point-in-time rule is applied.

The station panel preserves its data-availability and quality-control flags. Station moves, observing-time changes, and other recorded history events are retained as context; they do not create retrospective adjustments to the pre-registered selection protocol.

How to read the result

Distance can help diagnose geographic mismatch, but it is not the atlas's decision rule. A nearby station can still be a weak proxy when seasonal anomalies differ, and a more distant station can be selected when its trailing out-of-sample record is better. The national zero-distance table is included as a complementary check: proximity alone does not remove index basis.

The Nebraska material uses the same definitions, confidence conventions, and interpretation boundary as the national atlas. It does not use local market prices or claim that historical performance will repeat.
